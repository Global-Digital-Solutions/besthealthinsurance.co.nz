Health Insurance Declined? Getting Covered with Pre-Existing Conditions

Pre-existing conditions don't automatically disqualify you from health insurance. Here's how to navigate application requirements and find cover that works.

How Pre-Existing Conditions Affect Your Application

A pre-existing condition is any health issue you had before applying for insurance. The question isn't whether you can get cover — you can — it's what exclusions or conditions the insurer will apply.

Insurers in our market use different criteria. Some will exclude your condition entirely. Others will apply a waiting period (rare in health insurance unlike Australia). Most will accept your application but exclude that specific condition from cover.

The Critical First Step: Full Medical Disclosure

Your application asks about health history. You must be honest and complete — there's no such thing as a "small omission." Lying on your application gives the insurer grounds to cancel cover later or refuse claims.

If you had diabetes, depression, a knee injury, or cancer in the past, disclose it. The insurer already has access to your medical records through GP networks, so omitting it only harms you if you later need to claim.

Diabetes (Type 1 or 2): Most insurers accept this with a condition exclusion or minor loading. You're covered for everything except complications directly from your diabetes.

Heart conditions: Similar outcome — accepted but with specific exclusions for related conditions.

Cancer (past): Most insurers will exclude further cancer cover for 3-5 years post-treatment. Earlier treatment = longer exclusion period.

Mental health conditions: Partners Life and AIA are most accommodating. Southern Cross and nib are stricter. Accuro is moderate.

Arthritis and joint conditions: Usually accepted with no exclusion if managed.

Exclusion vs Waiting Periods vs Loading

An exclusion means the insurer won't cover that condition. A waiting period (rare in health insurance) means you can't claim for the condition within X months. A loading means your premium is higher than standard because of the condition.

Exclusions are most common. A $1,000 exclusion on your diabetes means if you need to see an endocrinologist, that visit isn't covered. A blanket exclusion on mental health cover means psychiatric admissions aren't covered.

Strategies to Get Better Terms

1. Shop multiple insurers: Partners Life, AIA, Accuro, and Southern Cross assess risk differently. You might get cover from one that another rejects.

2. Apply with your GP's letter: Getting your GP to write a letter confirming your condition is stable and well-managed improves your application chances.

3. Target insurers known for acceptance: AIA and Partners Life are most willing to accept applicants with pre-existing conditions. Southern Cross is stricter.

4. Accept the first offer, then appeal: If rejected, request the specific reason and appeal with additional medical evidence.

When Declining Your Application Is Likely

Insurers are most likely to decline applications where: - You're applying for cover on a condition you already have (e.g., applying for psychiatric cover while having active depression) - You've had multiple serious conditions and there's a pattern of risk - You've omitted information and it's discovered later

Most standard health insurance refusals can be overturned with persistence and full medical documentation.

The Long-Term Perspective

Getting cover with pre-existing conditions is absolutely possible. Don't give up after one rejection. Keep trying different insurers, work with your GP to document your health stability, and appeal decisions. Most Kiwis with chronic conditions successfully get health insurance with exclusions on their specific condition.
