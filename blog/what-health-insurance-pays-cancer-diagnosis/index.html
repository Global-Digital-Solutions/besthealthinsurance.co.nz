Diagnosed with Cancer? What Your Health Insurance Actually Pays

Cancer diagnosis triggers many costs beyond surgery: specialist consultations, imaging, non-surgical treatments, mental health support. Here's what insurance covers.

The Cancer Cost Reality

A cancer diagnosis sets off a complex financial journey. Many costs are covered by ACC or public health. Some are not. Insurance bridges critical gaps.

Total cancer journey costs (diagnosis through treatment to follow-up) can span $50,000-150,000+ depending on cancer type, stage, and treatment approach.

What Public/Pharmac Covers

Public healthcare covers: - Initial diagnostic imaging and pathology (biopsies, etc.) - Cancer surgery (hospital admission, surgeon fees) - Pharmac-funded chemotherapy and radiation - Ongoing public oncology consultations - Some psychological support (limited, waiting lists)

Out-of-pocket costs under public system: minimal to zero.

Private GP consultations: Not typically insured (not part of major medical tier) Private specialist consultations (pathologist, radiologist opinions): Insured if major medical Diagnostic imaging (private second opinion): Insured if major medical Biopsies: Typically covered

Cost if uninsured: $1,500-3,000 for pre-diagnosis workup Cost if insured: After excess (covered)

This is where major medical cover provides real value.

The Treatment Phase: Major Medical Costs

Once diagnosed, chemotherapy is Pharmac-covered and you pay minimal costs. But specialist consultations and management are covered by insurance:

Oncologist consultations: $400-500 per visit, insured (after excess) Medical oncology medication management: Some non-Pharmac drugs ($2,000-5,000/month), partially covered by insurance up to annual cap Pathology testing during treatment: Insured Imaging (CT, PET scans) for monitoring: Insured

Cost if uninsured: $15,000-30,000 for consultations and additional testing Cost if insured: Only excess (typically $500-1,000)

Mental Health During Cancer Treatment

This is often the most underestimated cost. Psychological impact of cancer diagnosis is significant.

Psychology consultations: $150-250 per session Psychiatric consultations (if medication needed): $300-400 per visit Typical course: Weekly psychology for 6 months (26 sessions) = $3,900-6,500

Without insurance: You either pay out-of-pocket or wait for public psychology (3-6 month waiting list) With insurance: Sessions are covered

This is where health insurance changes quality of life during treatment, not just financial cost.

Surgery: The Major Expense

Cancer surgery costs (if needed) are typically public system costs if you proceed through public treatment. If you choose private surgery:

Private surgeon fee: $5,000-15,000 (on top of hospital admission) Private hospital admission: $2,000-5,000 per day

With insurance: Surgery is covered under major medical Without insurance: $10,000-25,000 additional cost

Most people have cancer surgery publicly even if they've used private specialists for consultations.

Post-Treatment Follow-Up

After treatment completion, you're monitored for recurrence:

Specialist follow-up consultations: $400-500 quarterly for 2-3 years, then annual Monitoring imaging (CT, PET scans): $1,200-1,500 per scan, typically 1-2 per year post-treatment

Cost over 5 years of post-treatment monitoring: $10,000-15,000 With insurance: Covered after excess

The Annual Cap Trap

Health insurance policies have annual caps ($20,000-30,000 typically). Cancer treatment can exceed this.

Year 1 of cancer (diagnosis and initial treatment): $25,000 in insured services = hit cap, remaining costs out-of-pocket Year 2 of cancer (ongoing treatment, monitoring): New year, new cap applies = reset

So you're covered up to your annual limit each year, not a lifetime limit.

Understanding your specific policy's caps is essential.

Non-Pharmac Drug Coverage

If your cancer requires non-Pharmac drugs, insurance becomes critical. These cost thousands per month and insurance is the only practical way to access them.

Without insurance: You either can't afford non-Pharmac drugs or they consume entire family wealth With insurance: Partial or full coverage (depending on policy)

Critical Illness Insurance Impact

Some health insurance policies include critical illness add-on (pays a lump sum if you're diagnosed with cancer). This lump sum covers costs insurance doesn't pay for:

Out-of-pocket medications: $1,000-2,000 Complementary treatments: $2,000-5,000 Travel for specialist opinion: $1,000-3,000 Lost income during treatment: $10,000-50,000+ (if self-employed)

Critical illness lump sum: typically $50,000-150,000 This can make a real difference in financial survival during cancer treatment.

The Optimal Cancer Protection Strategy

1. Ensure major medical cover (specialist consultations must be covered) 2. Understand your annual cap and how it resets 3. Know whether non-Pharmac drugs are covered 4. Consider critical illness add-on for lump-sum protection 5. Ensure mental health support is available 6. Review your policy specifically for cancer coverage

Cancer is where health insurance truly matters. The difference between having insurance and not having it isn't about avoiding bankruptcy necessarily — it's about accessing the best treatment and not having cost decisions determine your care options.

If diagnosed with cancer, contact your insurer immediately (before treatment starts) to understand exactly what they'll cover and what you'll need to fund yourself.
